In detail

The craft behind the chair

Fades, decoded

Not sure which fade you want? A low fade keeps things subtle around the ears; a mid fade is the versatile all-rounder; a high fade gives the sharpest contrast. Tell us how you wear your hair day to day and how often you want to maintain it, and we'll match the fade to your lifestyle — not just the photo on your phone.

Beards, brought under control

A good beard trim is about architecture: weight removed where it bulks out, length kept where it adds shape, and lines that follow your face. We'll also show you how to keep it tidy between visits — which brush, which balm, and how often to oil it so it never looks neglected.

The hot towel ritual

Our shave isn't a rushed add-on — it's a ritual. Heat opens the pores and softens the bristles, the lather lifts each hair, and the razor does the rest in a single careful pass. If you've only ever shaved at the bathroom sink, this is the experience that converts you.

Grooming guide

Keep it golden between visits

A great cut deserves a little upkeep. Three habits that make the difference.

Fade upkeep

A skin fade looks its sharpest for the first two weeks. Book your tidy-up before it grows out completely — shorter intervals keep the blend looking intentional, and a quick neckline clean-up between cuts works wonders.

Beard basics

Brush it daily to train the growth direction, oil it after a warm shower when the hair is most receptive, and trim strays little and often rather than attempting a full reshape at home.

Bring a photo

Seen a style you like? Save it and show us. A photo starts the consultation better than any description — and we'll tell you honestly how it'll translate to your hair type.

Aftercare

The first week after your cut

A few small things keep a fresh cut looking fresh for longer.

Day one

Your cut is styled and finished when you leave — resist washing it the same evening if you can, so the style settles. A quick brush-through in the morning brings the shape back.

Through the week

Use the product we recommended, in the amount we showed you — most people use twice as much as they need. Apply to dry or towel-dried hair, work it through with your fingers first, then finish with a comb or brush.

When it's time

Fades announce themselves first at the neckline and around the ears. When those edges soften, that's your signal — message us and we'll get you back in the chair before the whole cut loses its shape.

Barber glossary

Talk like a regular

Barbering has its own language. A few terms you'll hear in the chair — so you can ask for exactly what you want.

Fade heights

Low, mid, high describes where the fade starts on the head. Low keeps it around the ears; high takes it up towards the crown for maximum contrast.

Guard numbers

Clipper guards run from 0 (closest) upwards. A "number 2 back and sides" leaves about 6mm; a skin fade works down through the guards to bare skin. Not sure? Describe the length in words — we'll translate.

Taper

A taper is a gentler, more gradual version of a fade, usually kept to the sideburns and neckline. The classic, understated finish for traditional cuts.

Texture

Texture is cut into the top with scissors or a razor to remove bulk and add movement, so hair sits naturally instead of lying flat and heavy.

Line-up

The sharpening of the hairline, temples and beard edges into clean, deliberate lines. It's the detail that makes a cut look freshly finished rather than nearly finished.

Neck shave

The finishing touch on most cuts: the neckline is edged and shaved clean with lather and a razor, so the back looks as sharp as the front.
