Style guide

Not sure what to ask for?

Half the battle with a haircut is knowing the words. Here is a plain-English guide to the styles we cut most often at Tranent Barbers — bring this page, a photo, or just a rough idea, and we will do the rest.

🎯

High, Mid or Low Fade — What's the Difference?

The "fade" is where the short back and sides blend up into the longer hair on top. A high fade takes that blend up high on the head for a sharp, striking contrast. A mid fade sits around the temples — the most popular all-rounder. A low fade keeps the blend down near the ears for a subtle, classic look that grows out the most gently. If you work somewhere smart, low or mid is usually the safe shout; if you want maximum sharpness, go high.

The Textured Crop

Short on top with choppy, textured length worn forward, usually paired with a fade on the back and sides. The crop is low-maintenance, suits most hair types — including thinning or receding hair, because the forward fringe covers a multitude of sins — and takes about thirty seconds to style in the morning with a fingertip of matte product worked through dry hair.

The Classic Scissor Cut

The gentleman's standard: cut mostly with scissors rather than clippers, leaving enough length to part, sweep back or tidy to the side. It is the most forgiving cut as it grows out, which makes it ideal if you cannot get to the barbers as often as you would like. Timeless, smart, and it works at sixteen or sixty.

Clipper Grades, Explained

Barbers talk in grades — the number is simply how short the clippers cut. A grade 1 is very short (about 3mm), grade 2 is roughly 6mm, grade 3 around 10mm, and grade 4 about 13mm. "Skin" or "zero" means the shortest possible finish at the bottom of a fade. If you have had a cut you loved, ask us to note the grades we used — next time, you can ask for exactly the same again.

Choosing a Beard Shape

A good beard follows the jaw you have. Fuller cheeks suit a squarer finish along the bottom; a softer jaw looks sharper with cleaner, straighter edges and a defined neckline just above the Adam's apple. The golden rule at home: never trim the neckline too high — it is the commonest beard mistake we fix. When we shape your beard in the shop, watch where the lines go so you can maintain them between visits.

Bringing a Photo? Read This First

Photos genuinely help — they show us the length, the finish and the overall shape you are picturing far faster than words can. Two honest tips: try to find a photo of someone with a similar hair type and hairline to yours, because the same cut sits differently on thick wavy hair than on fine straight hair; and be ready for honest advice. Between visits

Keeping your cut sharp at home

A great cut should still look great two weeks later. These simple habits, straight from the barber's chair, keep your hair and beard in shape between visits to the High Street.

✂

Know your grow-out

Hair grows roughly a centimetre a month. Skin fades look their sharpest for the first two to three weeks, so regulars tend to rebook on a cycle that suits their style — ask us in the chair what timing we would recommend for your cut, and message us on WhatsApp when you are ready.

💧

Less product, worked in properly

Most men use twice as much product as they need, in one lump at the front. Warm a pea-sized amount between your palms until it disappears, work it from the back of your head forwards, and add a little more only if you need it. For a natural finish choose matte products; for shine and slicker styles, choose a pomade-style finish.

🪮

Train it while it dries

Hair sets as it dries, so the two minutes after a shower matter more than ten minutes of styling later. Towel-dry gently, comb or brush your hair into the direction your cut is meant to sit, then leave it alone. Fighting a parting or a crown is usually a sign the cut underneath needs adjusting — tell us next visit and we will cut with your growth pattern, not against it.

🧴

Look after the skin under the beard

Itching and flaking under a beard is usually dry skin, not the beard itself. Wash the beard a couple of times a week, work a few drops of beard oil down to the skin (not just the hair) while it is still slightly damp, and brush it through daily to train the direction of growth and spread the oil evenly.

🚫

Leave the edges to us

The temptation to tidy your own neckline or edges at home is understandable — and the cause of most of the rescue jobs we see. One slip takes weeks to grow out. If an occasion comes up between cuts and you need to look sharp, message us first; a professional tidy-up is quicker, safer and keeps the shape of your cut intact.
