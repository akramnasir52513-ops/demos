Look after your cut

Simple tips between visits

A great cut deserves a little care at home. These basics keep your style looking fresh for longer.

Do not over-wash

Washing every day strips natural oils and can make hair dry and harder to style. Most hair types do better with a wash every two to three days.

Style on damp hair

Most products work best worked through towel-dried, slightly damp hair — start with a pea-sized amount and add more only if you need it.

Keep the edges tidy

Your cut looks its best for the first couple of weeks. A quick tidy-up of the neckline and edges between full cuts keeps everything looking sharp.

Care for your beard

Comb your beard daily to train the growth direction, and keep the skin underneath clean and moisturised — a healthy beard starts with healthy skin.

Show us a photo

Photos say what words cannot. Saving a picture of the style you like — or of your last cut when it looked its best — helps us match it exactly.

Book before you need it

Regular customers get the best slots. If you know your hair grows fast, message us a few days ahead so your preferred time is still available.

Your appointment

What happens when you sit in the chair

Never been to Sammi's before? Here is exactly how a typical appointment runs, so you know what to expect from the moment you walk in.

1

The consultation — we listen first

Every good haircut starts with a conversation, not clippers. We will ask what you have in mind, how you usually wear your hair, and how much time you spend styling it each morning. If you have a photo of the style you like, show us — it helps us match your idea precisely. We will be honest if a style needs a bit of adapting for your hair type, and we will explain why before anything is cut, so there are no surprises halfway through.

2

The cut — careful, checked work

Once we agree on the plan, the cut itself is methodical: sections worked in order, lengths checked against each other, and blends built up gradually rather than taken down all at once. For fades, that means the blend is refined in stages until it disappears smoothly into the top. For scissor cuts, it means your hair is checked dry as well as wet, because hair behaves differently — and looks different — once it is dry.

3

The detail work — edges, lines and neckline

This is the part that separates a tidy cut from a great one. We clean up the hairline, sharpen the edges around the ears and neckline, tidy sideburns, and — if you are having beard work — shape and balance the beard so it works with the haircut rather than fighting it. A hot towel finish softens the skin and makes the razor detailing comfortable and precise.

4

The finish — styled and explained

Before you leave the chair, we style your hair the way you would wear it day to day and show you the back and sides in the mirror so you can check everything yourself. We will talk you through how to recreate the finish at home — which product type suits your hair, how much to use, and the order to apply it — so day two looks as good as day one. If anything does not feel right, say so there and then: we would far rather adjust it in the chair than have you leave unsure.

Style guide

Words you will hear in the shop — explained

Barbers have their own language. Here is a plain-English guide to the terms that come up most, so you can ask for exactly what you want with confidence.

Skin fade

The sides and back are blended down to the skin at the bottom, rising gradually into longer hair above. Ask for a high, mid or low fade depending on where you want the blend to start.

Taper

A gentler version of a fade: the hair gradually shortens only around the edges — the sideburns and neckline — for a softer, more classic finish that suits the office as much as the weekend.

Textured crop

Short on top with choppy, textured length that falls forward, usually paired with a fringe. Low-maintenance, modern, and great for thicker hair that needs the weight taken out.

Buzz cut grades

Clipper grades run from 0 (closest) upward — each grade is roughly 3mm of length. A grade 3 on top with faded sides is a common, tidy starting point if you are going shorter.

Beard shaping

More than a trim: the beard's outline is defined — cheek lines cleaned, neckline set at the right height, and bulk removed where it grows widest so your jaw looks its sharpest.

Growing a cut out

Tell us if you are growing your hair longer. We will tidy the shape without taking off the length you have worked for, and advise how often a tidy-up will keep it looking intentional.

Still not sure which of these is you? That is completely normal — bring the question to your appointment and we will work it out together in the consultation.

Families & hair types

Kids' first cuts, and care for every hair type

Because Sammi's is unisex, we look after the whole family's hair in one place. Here is what parents ask us most, and how we approach different hair types in the chair.

A calm first haircut

A child's first trip to the barbers sets the tone for years, so we keep it gentle: no rush, everything explained before it happens, and the noisy clippers introduced slowly. If your wee one would rather start with just a fringe tidy, that is absolutely fine — confidence first, haircut second.

Bring them at a good time

Mid-morning or early afternoon usually works best for younger children — after food, before tiredness. If you are booking for a child, mention it on WhatsApp and we will suggest the quieter times of day.

Thick & wavy hair

Thick hair needs its weight managed, not just shortened. We remove bulk from where it builds up, keep enough length for the natural wave to sit properly, and show you how a small amount of product stops it puffing out by midday.

Fine & thinning hair

With fine hair, the right cut makes all the difference: blunt edges make ends look fuller, overly long tops can look thinner, and a slightly shorter, textured style usually gives the fullest impression. We will advise honestly on what will work.

Curly hair

Curls shrink as they dry, so we cut curly hair with that spring in mind — never judging the length while it is stretched or soaking wet. The result sits at the length you actually asked for once it is dry and natural.

Straight hair

Straight hair shows every line, which means it rewards precision. We check symmetry carefully, soften any harsh corners, and choose finishes — matte rather than shiny products, for example — that keep it looking natural rather than styled within an inch of its life.

Before you visit

Three small things that make your appointment better

✓

Arrive with your hair as it is

Come with your hair in its normal state — not freshly washed with heavy product unless that is genuinely how you wear it. Seeing how your hair naturally sits helps us cut it to behave that way at home, and tell us honestly how long it has been since your last cut so we can judge the growth pattern.

✓

Save a photo or two

A photo of the style you want — or of your own hair on a day it looked great — is worth a long description. Equally, a photo of what you do NOT want helps just as much. Keep them on your phone and show us at the consultation; two minutes spent there saves guesswork later.

✓

Speak up in the chair

Prefer it shorter on the sides? Nervous about the fringe? Tell us while we are cutting, not after. We check in as we go, and a small adjustment mid-cut is always easy — our only goal is that you leave the shop happy with what you see in the mirror.
