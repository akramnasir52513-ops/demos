In Detail

What actually happens in the chair

No mysteries and no rushing. Here is how each of the main services runs, from the moment you sit down to the moment you check the mirror.

Step by StepThe Haircut

It starts with a conversation: what you want, how your hair grows, and how much styling you realistically do in the morning. The cut is then built with clipper and scissor work matched to your hair type — thick, fine, straight or wavy all behave differently, and the technique follows the hair, not a template.

The detail work is where a good cut separates itself: clean edges around the ears, a neckline that sits right, and a fringe that falls where it should. You finish styled, with a clear look at the back and sides before you leave the chair.

Step by StepThe Skin Fade

First, the height: high, mid or low, chosen against your head shape and the style on top. The barber then works through the grades, blending each one into the next until the transition disappears — checking the blend in the mirror as it develops, because a fade can look right from the front and wrong from the side.

Edges are sharpened last, the top is cut and styled to sit with the fade, and the whole thing is checked once more under the shop light. If a line is visible, it is not finished — that is the rule.

Step by StepBeard Trim & Hot Towel Shave

For a beard trim, the shape is agreed first — how much length comes off, where the cheek line sits, and how the neckline should run. Length is evened, lines are sharpened, and a hot towel softens everything where needed before the finishing oil or balm goes on.

For the full hot towel shave, towels open the pores and soften the bristles, a protective lather is worked up, and the straight razor takes it close in careful passes. A cold towel closes everything down, and balm leaves the skin calm rather than raw.

Step by StepKids' Cuts

Young customers get time, not pressure. The style is agreed with the parent first, the pace stays relaxed, and the cut itself is done to the same standard as any gents cut — tidy edges, even length, and a finish they can wear to school with pride.

First Visit?

How to get the most from your appointment

A little preparation makes a good cut even better. Four simple things regulars at barber shops everywhere swear by — they work just as well for your first visit to Taibach Barbers.

Tip OneBring a photo

A picture of the style you want is the fastest way to make sure you and your barber are picturing the same haircut. Screenshots are fine — show them at the consultation, and the barber will tell you honestly how the style will work with your hair type and growth pattern, and adjust it where it needs adjusting.

Tip TwoKnow your last cut

If you liked your last haircut, remembering the grade used on the back and sides — a two into a fade, a scissor cut all over, and so on — gives the barber a head start. If you did not like it, say what bothered you. Both are useful; barbers would always rather know.

Tip ThreeTime a fresh fade right

A skin fade looks its absolute sharpest in its first week. If the cut is for a wedding, interview, holiday or photos, book a few days before the event rather than the morning of it — the style settles, you get used to it, and there is room for a small adjustment if you want one. Message on WhatsApp to line the date up.

Tip FourSpeak up in the chair

The mirror check at the end is not a formality. If you want the fringe shorter, the sides tighter or the beard line lower, say so while you are still in the chair — small adjustments take a minute then, and the whole point is that you leave happy with what you see.

What is the difference between a haircut and a skin fade?

A standard haircut keeps a more even, natural length through the back and sides, usually with more scissor work. A skin fade is taken down to the skin at the bottom and blended up through the grades — high, mid or low — for a sharper, more graduated look. Not sure which suits you? The consultation at the start of your appointment will sort it.

What happens during a hot towel shave?

Hot towels soften your skin and bristles first, then a rich lather and a straight razor give you the closest possible shave. A cold towel closes the pores afterwards, and a soothing balm finishes the job — clean, smooth and calm.

Do you cut children's hair?

What if I am not sure what style suits me?

That is exactly what the consultation is for. Tell the barber what you like, how your hair behaves and how much styling you do each day, and you will be pointed towards a cut or fade that works with your hair — not against it. You can also bring a photo of a style you like; it always helps.
