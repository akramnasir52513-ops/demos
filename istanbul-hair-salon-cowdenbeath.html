Your visit

What happens during your appointment

New to Istanbul Hair Salon, or new to Turkish barbering altogether? Here is exactly what to expect from the moment you walk through the door on Cowdenbeath High Street.

1

The consultation

Every appointment starts with a conversation. Your barber will ask what you are after, look at your hair type and growth pattern, and agree the style with you before a single clipper comes out. If you have a photo of the look you want, show it — it always helps. If you are not sure, your barber will recommend a cut that suits your face shape and lifestyle, and explain how it will grow out.

2

The cut

This is where the craft shows. Fades are blended by hand in stages, scissor work is done section by section, and your barber will keep checking the shape from every angle as they work. Turkish barbering is known for its patience — we would rather take an extra minute on the blend than rush the finish, because the details are what people notice.

3

The hot towel finish

If you are having a shave or beard sculpt, this is the heart of the ritual: warm towels soften the hair and skin, a rich lather is worked in, and the straight razor does its work in smooth, careful passes. Even on a standard haircut, expect the neckline and edges to be cleaned up with the razor for that crisp, just-barbered outline.

4

Style, check & go

Before you leave the chair, your cut is washed down or brushed off, styled the way you would wear it day to day, and checked in the mirror from the front, sides and back. Your barber will show you how to recreate the style at home and let you know roughly when to come back to keep it sharp. Then you are on your way — and when you are ready to rebook, one WhatsApp message is all it takes.

From the chair

Keeping your cut sharp between visits

A great haircut should keep looking great for weeks. A few simple habits, straight from the barber's chair, make all the difference.

Book by how it grows, not by the calendar

Short fades usually look their best for two to three weeks, while longer scissor cuts can go longer between visits. Watch the neckline and the area around the ears — when those start to look untidy, it is time. Booking your next appointment on WhatsApp while your current cut still looks good means you never end up waiting for a slot when you need one most.

Less product, applied properly

Most styling problems come from using too much product on dry hair. Start with a pea-sized amount, warm it between your palms, and work it through slightly damp hair from the back forwards. You can always add a little more — you cannot take it away without washing it out.

Look after the beard daily

A beard sculpt in the salon sets the shape, but daily care keeps it. Comb it through after a shower while it is soft, keep the neckline where your barber set it rather than creeping higher, and if the edges between appointments bother you, a tidy-up visit is quicker than a full restyle.

Tell your barber what did not work

If your last cut grew out unevenly, felt too heavy on top, or was hard to style in the morning, say so at your next consultation. That feedback is genuinely useful — it tells your barber exactly what to adjust, and it is how a good haircut becomes your haircut, refined a little more every visit.
