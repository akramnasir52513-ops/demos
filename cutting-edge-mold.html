In the chair

Your appointment, step by step

New to the shop? Here is exactly how a visit to Cutting Edge runs, from the moment you sit down to the moment you check the mirror and nod.

1 — The consultation

Every appointment starts with a conversation, not the clippers. You will be asked what you are after, how you have been wearing your hair, and what has or has not worked for you before. If you have a photo, show it. If the style you want will not behave with your hair type, the barber will say so honestly and suggest the closest version that will actually work day to day. Nothing starts until you are both clear on the plan.

2 — The cut

With the plan agreed, the cut itself is done with care rather than speed. Fades are built up gradually and checked from every angle as they go, scissor work is shaped to your head rather than a template, and the barber will keep checking in as the shape comes together. If something looks different to what you pictured, say so mid-cut — small adjustments are easy while you are in the chair.

3 — The detail work

This is where a good cut becomes a sharp one. Edges are lined up clean around the ears and neck, the neckline is finished properly, stray weight is taken out so the style sits right, and beard work — if you are having it — is shaped and balanced to the cut rather than treated as an afterthought. For hot towel shaves, this is the full traditional sequence: hot towels, lather, the shave itself, and a cooling finish.

4 — The finish & the mirror check

Your hair is finished and styled so you can see the cut as it is meant to look — not just how it falls straight out of the chair. You get the mirror check from the front, sides and back before the cape comes off. Then, before you leave, you will get simple advice on keeping the style right at home: how to dry it, what to do on day two, and when to come back for a tidy-up to keep it sharp.

Barber's advice

Keeping your cut sharp between visits

A great cut should still look good weeks later. These are the simple habits the chair would tell you about — free advice, whether you book or not.

Tip 01

Book the tidy-up early

Fades look their best in the first couple of weeks. If you like yours sharp, get the next tidy-up in the diary before the fade grows out completely — a short clip-back between full cuts is quicker and keeps the shape the barber built for you.

Tip 02

Dry before you style

Most styles are set while the hair dries, not after. Towel-dry, push the hair in the direction you want it to sit, and let it settle. Two minutes of direction while it dries does more than ten minutes of fixing it once it is dry and set the wrong way.

Tip 03

Less product, worked in properly

A pea-sized amount, rubbed warm between your palms and worked from the back of the head forward, beats a big blob dumped on top. Too much product is the most common reason a fresh cut looks heavy by lunchtime.

Tip 04

Beards need lines, not length

A beard reads as tidy or scruffy mostly by its lines — cheeks and neckline — not its length. If you are growing yours out, a quick shape-up visit keeps it looking deliberate instead of accidental while the length comes in.

Tip 05

Sleep on it before you judge it

Hair sits differently the morning after a cut than it does in the chair. Give a new style a wash and one full day before deciding — and if something still is not right, message the shop and ask. Small tweaks are a normal part of settling into a new cut.

Tip 06

Photos help the next cut too

When your cut looks exactly how you wanted it, take a quick photo. Show it at your next appointment and the barber can rebuild the same shape precisely, instead of working from memory — yours or theirs.
