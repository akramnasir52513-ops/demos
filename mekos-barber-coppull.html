In detail

The craft, explained

Not sure what to ask for? This is the plain-English version of what happens in the chair — so you can book with confidence and know exactly what you're getting.

Fades, decoded

A fade is simply hair that graduates from very short at the bottom to longer at the top, with no visible line where one length meets the next. The name describes where the fade starts: a low fade begins just above the ear and keeps more weight around the sides — the most forgiving, classic option. A mid fade starts higher, around temple level, and suits most head shapes. A high fade takes the short work right up towards the crown for the sharpest contrast.

A skin fade takes the lowest part right down to the skin before blending up. Whichever you choose, the blend is built in stages and checked under proper light — a fade is only finished when it looks smooth from the front, the sides and the back.

Beard shaping, properly

A beard trim is not just "shorter". Shaping means the length is balanced so the beard holds its outline, the cheek lines are set at a height that suits your face, and the neckline is placed so it looks clean from the front — not cut so high it doubles your chin, nor left so low it looks untidy.

We work with how your beard actually grows: filling the shape where it's full, easing off where it's patchier, and lining up the edges with a razor finish. The result is a beard that looks grown on purpose. If you're growing yours out, regular small shapings on the way beat waiting until it's wild and losing length to a rescue job.

The hot towel ritual

The hot towel shave is the oldest service in barbering, and it has survived because nothing else gets as close or feels as good. Hot towels soften the beard and open the pores; a rich lather lifts the hairs; then the straight razor works with the grain first and across it second, stretching the skin gently for a smooth pass without dragging.

A cold towel at the end closes everything down and settles the skin. It's the finish to choose before a big occasion — or for no occasion at all, just because half an hour of being looked after properly is a rare thing.

Your first visit

New to the shop? Here's how it goes

Walking into a new barber shop can feel like a gamble — you've probably had the "that isn't what I asked for" experience somewhere before. Here's exactly what happens at Mekoś Barber, so you know what you're booking.

First, we talk. Before any clippers come out, we'll ask what you normally have, what you liked or didn't like about your last cut, and how you style it day to day. A photo helps if you have one; if not, plain words work fine. If what you're asking for won't sit right on your hair — wrong length for the growth pattern, a fade height that will fight your crown — we'll say so and show you the version that will work.

Then the cut itself, finished properly: edges tidied, neckline cleaned up, and a final look in the mirror from the front, sides and back. If you want a tweak, say the word — adjustments in the chair are part of the service, not an awkward extra. That's the standard that's earned the shop its 4.9 stars from 40 Google reviews, and it applies to your first visit exactly as it does to a ten-year regular's.

Bringing a photo

From the chair

Looking after your cut & beard

The cut is half the job — the other half is the few minutes you spend on it at home. Straight advice from the shop floor:

Make the fade last

A skin fade looks its sharpest in the first fortnight. Sleeping on it flattens one side faster than the other, so a quick rinse and a brush back into place in the morning resets it. When the blend starts to blur into the top — usually when strangers stop asking if you've just had it done — it's refresh time.

Product: less than you think

A pea-sized amount, warmed between the palms and worked through dry or towel-dry hair, beats a handful slapped on wet hair every time. Apply to the roots of the top first, then shape. If your hair feels crunchy or looks greasy by lunchtime, you've used too much — halve it tomorrow.

Beard care basics

Wash your beard with the same care as the hair on your head — a gentle wash a few times a week, not daily stripping. Comb it through after a shower to train the growth direction, and keep the cheek and neck lines tidy between visits with careful, small trims rather than one big correction.

Know your refresh rhythm

Short fades want attention every two to three weeks to stay sharp; scissor cuts and longer styles can stretch further. Book your next slot before you leave the shop or over WhatsApp while it's in your mind — the diary fills fastest at weekends and before bank holidays.
