The Craft

How a Karizma cut comes together

A great haircut isn't luck — it's process. Here's what happens between you sitting down and you checking the mirror.

Fades, decoded

A fade is built in stages — shortest at the bottom, blended up through the grades so there are no steps and no harsh lines. Where it sits changes everything: high fades are bold and sharp, low fades are softer and classic, tapers just clean up the edges.

Not sure which is yours? Describe your routine — two minutes of styling or twenty — and you'll be matched to a cut that fits your life, not just the photo.

The finish is the difference

Edges cleaned, hairline sharpened, neckline right — the last five minutes are what separate a good cut from a Karizma cut. Then it's styled in front of you, so you see it at its best and learn exactly how to recreate it at home.

You'll leave knowing what product was used, how much, and when to come back — most fades want a refresh every two to three weeks.

First visit? Here's what to expect

Message on WhatsApp or book through Nearcut, arrive at your time, and take the chair. First comes the chat — what you have now, what you want, what hasn't worked before. Then the cut. No jargon, no pressure, no surprises at the till — the price is confirmed before the clippers start.

What's the difference between a fade and a taper?

A skin fade blends the back and sides down to the skin and can sit high, mid or low. A taper keeps more length and just cleans up around the ears and neckline — a softer, more classic finish. Not sure? Ask in the chair and you'll be talked through it.

What does a hot towel shave involve?

Hot towels open the pores and soften the beard, a rich lather cushions the blade, and a straight razor gives a closer shave than a cartridge can. A cold towel and soothing finish close everything back down — the traditional barber's shave, done properly.

Look After It

Keeping it sharp between visits

A great cut deserves a little care at home. These are the habits that keep a Karizma cut looking fresh for longer.

Fade upkeep

A skin fade looks its best in the first fortnight. Keep the neckline clean between visits, sleep on a clean pillowcase to protect the edges, and rebook every two to three weeks — a fade left six weeks isn't a trim any more, it's a whole new haircut.

Short on time? A tidy-up of the edges and neckline between full cuts keeps you presentable in half the chair time.

Beard care basics

Wash your beard with shampoo, not face soap — the skin underneath will thank you. Comb it daily in the direction you want it to grow, and resist trimming the shape yourself between visits; strays are fine to snip, but the cheek line and neckline are best left to the chair.

Dry, wiry beard? Ask in the shop about oils and balms — the right product takes ten seconds a day.

Styling in two minutes

Most home styling fails for one reason: too much product, on dry hair, all at once. Use half what you think you need, warm it between your palms, and work it through towel-dried hair from the back to the front — the front gets what's left, which is exactly enough.

Every Karizma cut is finished in front of you, so you see the technique before you try it yourself.
