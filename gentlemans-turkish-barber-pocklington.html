In Detail

The Turkish Barber Difference

The Hot Towel Ritual

A hot towel is not just for show. It softens coarse beard hair, opens the pores and prepares the skin, so the razor glides instead of dragging. That is why a traditional Turkish shave feels closer and leaves skin calmer than a quick shave at home.

After the shave, a cool towel closes everything back down and a soothing balm leaves your skin refreshed — no burn, no tightness.

Fades That Grow Out Well

Anyone can take the sides short. A well-built fade is blended in stages so there are no visible lines on the day — and no awkward step appearing a week later. We blend with the grain of your hair growth and check the blend under the shop lights from every angle.

Tell us how often you like to come in, and we will build the fade to suit your routine.

The Razor Finish

It is the last two minutes that make a cut look finished: the hairline, around the ears and the back of the neck, all cleaned up with the cut-throat razor. It is a small detail you will notice every morning in the mirror until your next visit.

The Tradition

Why Turkish Barbering?

Barbering in Turkey is one of the oldest crafts in daily life — for centuries, the barber's shop has been a place where a gentleman is properly looked after, not simply trimmed. The tradition built its reputation on three things: patience, precision, and ritual.

Patience, because a proper cut cannot be hurried — the blend is checked, the shape is balanced, and the customer does not leave until it is right. Precision, because the tools of the trade — the cut-throat razor above all — reward a steady, practised hand and punish a careless one. And ritual, because the hot towels, the lather and the finishing touches turn a necessity into an experience worth repeating.

That is the standard we hold ourselves to at 65 Market Street, Pocklington: a traditional service, done the traditional way, for the gentlemen of this town and the villages around it. If you have never tried it, your first hot towel shave will explain everything words cannot.

Aftercare

After Your Appointment

After a Hot Towel Shave

Your skin has had a close shave, so treat it gently for the rest of the day: splash with cool water, pat dry rather than rubbing, and use a gentle, alcohol-free balm if you have one. Avoid heavy fragrances on the shaved area that day, and give your skin a day's rest before shaving again at home.

After a Skin Fade

A fresh fade can make your scalp a little sun-sensitive for the first day or two — worth remembering in summer. Keep the hair clean, avoid scratching the freshly clipped areas, and your skin will settle quickly. If you notice the edges softening sooner than you would like, book your tidy-up a few days earlier next time.

Barber Glossary

Words You Will Hear in the Chair

Fade

A cut where the hair gradually shortens from the top of the sides down towards the skin, with no visible line between the lengths. A "skin fade" takes the shortest part right down to the skin.

Taper

A gentler version of a fade — the hair shortens gradually around the neckline and sideburns while keeping more length overall. The classic, understated finish.

Cut-Throat Razor

The traditional straight razor used for hot towel shaves and for cleaning up hairlines and edges. In trained hands it gives the closest finish of any blade.

Grade

The clipper guard number that sets the length: the lower the grade, the shorter the cut. If you know your grade from a previous cut, tell us — it helps us match it exactly.

Line-Up

Sharpening the natural edges of the haircut — the hairline, temples and around the ears — with the razor, so the whole cut looks crisp and finished.

Hot Towel

A towel heated and placed over the face before a shave. It softens the beard and opens the pores, making the shave closer and kinder to the skin.

Grooming Guide

Keeping Sharp Between Visits

How Often Should You Come In?

A skin fade looks its best for around two to three weeks before the blend starts to soften. A classic scissor cut holds its shape longer — usually four to six weeks. If you are growing your hair out, regular tidy-ups keep it looking intentional rather than untidy while it grows.

Beards benefit from a professional shape every few weeks, even if you trim at home in between — the lines around the cheeks and neck are what keep a beard looking deliberate.

Looking After a Fade at Home

Wash your hair regularly but avoid stripping it with very hot water, which dries the scalp. A small amount of product worked through dry hair gives a fade texture without weight. If the edges start to look untidy before your next appointment, resist tidying them yourself — a two-minute razor finish in the shop keeps the lines exactly where they belong.

Beard Care Basics

Rinse your beard daily and wash it properly a few times a week — ordinary shampoo can be harsh, so be gentle. Comb it through after washing to train the direction of growth, and trim stray hairs rather than cutting into the shape. Dry skin underneath is the most common cause of itch; keeping the skin clean and moisturised makes a real difference.

Shaving at Home, the Barber's Way

The hot towel ritual works at home too: shave after a warm shower, or hold a warm, damp towel against your face for a minute first. Use short strokes, rinse the blade often, and finish with cold water and a gentle balm. Your skin will notice the difference straight away.

Choosing Your Next Style

Thinking of a change? The best styles work with your natural hair — its thickness, its growth pattern and how much time you want to spend on it each morning. Bring your ideas to your appointment and we will talk through what will genuinely suit you before anything is cut.
