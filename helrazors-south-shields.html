The Fade, Explained

How a HelRAZORS Fade Is Built

A fade is not one clipper guard — it is a sequence. Here is how the blend in your chair actually happens:

  • 1Set the guideline. The fade height is agreed first — high, mid or low — and cut in clean.
  • 2Build the blend. Guards are worked up in stages so the gradient has no visible steps.
  • 3Detail the edges. Hairline, temples and neckline are sharpened with trimmers and the razor.
  • 4Check it twice. The cut is checked in the mirror from every side before any product goes near it.
The Ritual

The Hot Towel Shave, Step by Step

Some things should never be rushed. The traditional shave at HelRAZORS follows the same ritual barbers have trusted for generations:

  • 1Hot towel prep. Steamed towels open the pores and soften the beard for a closer pass.
  • 2Lather & first pass. A rich lather is worked in, then a steady straight-razor pass with the grain.
  • 3Second pass & detail. Re-lathered and finished against the grain for a smooth result, edges tidied.
  • 4Cold towel finish. A cold towel closes everything down, followed by a soothing skin finish.

Know Before You Sit Down

Popular Styles, Explained

Not sure what to ask for? Here is a plain-English guide to the cuts lads ask HelRAZORS for most — show your barber the one you mean, or just describe it and we will translate.

The Skin Fade

The sides and back are clipped down to the skin at the base and blended up into the length on top, so there is no visible line where the short meets the long. Ask for it high if you want the blend to start above the temples for a sharper, bolder look, mid for the classic all-rounder, or low if you prefer a softer, more natural grow-out. Fades look their best fresh, so most lads re-book every two to three weeks to keep the blend crisp — message us on WhatsApp and we will keep your slot regular.

Pairs well with: a crop or textured fringe on top, a quiff, or a slicked-back finish. If your hairline is receding, a mid fade with a shorter, textured top usually works better than length that draws the eye up — ask in the chair and you will get an honest opinion.

The Classic Scissor Cut

Timeless, tidy and office-safe: length kept on the sides with scissors rather than clippers, a natural taper around the ears and neckline, and enough length on top to part it, sweep it or leave it loose. It grows out gracefully, which makes it the smart choice if you cannot get to the shop as often.

Ask for a taper if you want the very edges faded softly while keeping scissor length above, or keep it fully scissored for the softest finish. A scissor cut also suits finer or thinning hair better than an aggressive fade, because the extra length keeps coverage where you want it.

The Shaped Beard

A good beard is architecture, not accident. The bulk is taken down evenly so it does not flare at the sides, the cheek line is set where it flatters your face, and the neckline is carved so it looks deliberate from every angle — including from below, which is the angle everyone else actually sees. Finished with a hot towel so the skin underneath is looked after too.

Growing one out? Book a tidy every few weeks anyway: keeping the lines sharp while the length comes in is the difference between "growing a beard" and "forgot to shave". Ask in the chair for product advice for your beard type — no obligation, just straight answers.
